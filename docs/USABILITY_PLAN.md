# Sala Usability Plan

This plan is for quick moderated sessions using only synthetic data. Do not use real patient names, real appointment details, or any real health information.

## Research Questions

- Can staff complete an arrival check-in without asking for the same information twice?
- Can a patient find their token on the board without needing their name called?
- Does the privacy lens help staff understand what the room can see?
- Do participants understand that wait ranges are rough estimates, not promises?
- Does any copy imply HIPAA compliance or production readiness?

## Participants

Run 2 to 3 sessions:

- 1 person who can act as front-desk staff.
- 1 person who can act as a patient in the waiting room.
- Optional: 1 person who reviews the case study as a hiring manager or designer.

## Consent Script

I am testing a prototype with synthetic data only. Please do not share real medical information. I am testing the design, not you. You can stop at any time. I may take notes about what you do and say, but I will not include your name in the write-up.

## Tasks

1. Check in a synthetic patient named Lina Campos with a follow-up visit and Spanish as the preferred language.
2. Read the confirmation token aloud as if speaking to a patient.
3. On the staff queue, change one waiting patient to called.
4. Turn on “See what the room sees” and explain what changed.
5. On the board, find the token and describe when the patient should expect to be called.
6. Review the case-study boundary graphic and explain what data the board is forbidden to show.

## Success Signals

- Participant completes check-in without moderator help.
- Participant can identify the public token on the board.
- Participant can explain that the board does not show names, DOB, or visit reasons.
- Participant describes the wait range as approximate.
- Participant notices the privacy lens and understands why private fields disappear.
